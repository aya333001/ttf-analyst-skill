# TTF scoring rubric: 21 dimensions

Based on the Task-Technology Fit framework (Goodhue and Thompson, 1995). Use every item, in three groups of seven.

## 1. Task characteristics: what the work demands

- **Task complexity:** how structured the decisions are (structured, semi-structured or unstructured).
- **Task interdependence:** how many people, teams or systems the work passes through.
- **Task variability:** whether the work is routine, varies case by case, or is unpredictable.
- **Task frequency:** how often the work is done, and in what volume.
- **Task criticality:** what goes wrong for the organization if the work is done badly.
- **Data requirements:** the kinds of data involved (structured, unstructured, live).
- **Required skills/knowledge:** the expertise and mental effort the work takes.

## 2. Technology characteristics: what the tool brings

- **Functionality:** what the tool can actually do (process data, analyze, communicate, automate).
- **Usability:** how easily the intended users can pick it up and use it.
- **Compatibility:** how well it fits the systems and workflow already in place.
- **Flexibility/adaptability:** how far it can be configured to this particular task.
- **Accessibility:** whether the right people can reach it when and where they need it.
- **Data handling capabilities:** whether it copes with the data types and volumes involved.
- **Security & compliance:** whether it meets privacy, policy and regulatory requirements.

## 3. Fit dimensions: how well the two match

- **Task support:** whether the tool directly helps get the work done or the decision made.
- **Performance improvement potential:** expected gains in speed, quality, cost or satisfaction.
- **Cognitive fit:** whether the tool presents information the way users already think about the work.
- **Process fit:** whether it slots into, or improves, the existing process.
- **User acceptance potential:** whether users are likely to adopt it, given usefulness and ease of use.
- **Proof-of-concept feasibility:** whether a small prototype could show the fit.
- **Proof-of-value evidence:** whether a pilot could show stakeholders measurable value.
